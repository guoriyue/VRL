# Why the Qwen-Image-2.1 aesthetic GRPO runs did not improve anything

Debug note, 2026-09-27, written after `STOP_SUMMARY_20260927.md`. No training or evaluation was resumed; the only new
compute is a 192-image sampler diagnostic (below), outputs in `/mnt/nvme/aesdiag/`.

## Verdict

The runs could not have learned much, for two independent reasons. Lowering the learning rate addressed neither.

1. **The advantage was almost entirely noise for the trained actions (credit assignment).**
2. **The reward was never qualified.** Its own card says "ready for a training key: no".

## 1. Credit assignment: the policy controls less than 1% of the within-group reward spread

The training sampler (`window2_trial/train_to_60/resolved_config.yaml`):

- 1024 px, 40 steps.
- Flow-GRPO SDE with `noise_level: 0.2`, `sde.window_size: 2` and `window_range: [4, 36]`: 2 stochastic steps, ODE everywhere else.
- Only those 2 steps are trained.
- `rollout.group_shared_noise` unset (default false), so every sample in a group starts from a **different initial latent**.
- `algorithm.global_std: true`.

The diagnostic uses 8 training prompts × 8 samples at exactly this sampler. It compares distinct initial latents (as trained)
with a shared initial latent (DanceGRPO / Flash-GRPO iso-grouping, `rollout.group_shared_noise: true`). The SDE-window seed is
the same within a group in both arms. Scores come from the same SigLIP V2.5 head (revision pinned as in `scorer.yaml`).

| arm | mean within-group std of aesthetic score |
|---|---|
| A: distinct initial noise, noise 0.2 (**as trained**) | **0.366** |
| B: shared initial noise, noise 0.2 | 0.033 |
| C: shared initial noise, noise 0.7 | 0.074 |
| reference: std over all A samples (across prompts) | 0.754 |

- **The trained steps explain about 0.8% of the variance.** (0.033 / 0.366)² ≈ 0.8%. The other 99% comes from the initial
  latent, which the policy does not choose and the log-prob of the 2 trained steps does not see. GRPO therefore regressed the
  2-step log-probs on essentially random advantages. This matches the observed KL of ~1e-5, the clip fraction mostly 0, and
  held-out aesthetic deltas whose CIs all include 0.
- **Visual check** (`/mnt/nvme/aesdiag/montage.jpg`, one prompt, rows A/B/C): A shows eight different compositions. B and C
  show eight near-identical images even at noise 0.7. At this sampler the policy can only make imperceptible changes.
- **`global_std: true` shrinks the usable signal further.** The advantage is divided by the batch std (~0.75, dominated by
  between-prompt differences), not the group std (~0.37 here, and ~0.03 of it attributable to the policy). With Adam the global
  scale largely cancels, but the KL term (`kl_coef 0.04`) keeps its size. KL therefore dominates relative to the tiny real
  signal and pins the policy to the base.

The qualification report's spread check (mean within-group range 0.837) was measured at 512 px / 10 steps, with every step
stochastic at noise 0.7. There, spread is policy-attributable. It does not transfer to the 2-step, noise-0.2 training sampler.
Reward-card gate 4 ("spread under the training sampler") was never run, and it would have caught this.

## 2. The reward failed its qualification and was trained on anyway

`reward_card.md` / `REPORT.md` (2026-09-25):

- **Blind preference gate failed.** Aesthetic-selected vs random: 57.0% utility, 95% CI 46.1–68.0%.
- **Label agreement failed.** AUC 0.523.
- **Shortcut checks failed.** Blur scored at least the genuine image on 38% of sources, black on 11%, and pixel noise on 12%.
- **Composition/counting is anti-correlated.** On that subset, preference utility was 18.8%.

`REPORT.md` concludes "do not train this recipe yet" and states that no RL was launched. The later LoRA campaign trained on the
same reward regardless. Even with perfect optimization, a higher SigLIP score is not established to be a visible improvement.
The fixed-64 aesthetic deltas (+0.04 to +0.07, CIs across 0) and the PickScore regression at checkpoint 60 are consistent with that.

## What to change before any further run

1. **Share initial noise within a group**: set `rollout.group_shared_noise: true`. The group advantage then compares only what
   the trained steps did.
2. **Give the trained steps real control.** Raise `noise_level` (0.7–1.0), put the window over early, high-noise steps (for
   example `window_range: [0, 12]`) and/or widen it. Then **measure** within-group reward spread under the exact training
   sampler with shared noise (gate 4). Arm C (noise 0.7, window [4, 36]) is still only 0.074, so the window placement matters.
3. **Normalize per group** (`algorithm.global_std: false`), or verify that the policy term is not dominated by KL.
4. **Re-qualify the reward** at the sampler and resolution that training and deployment will use. Without a passing
   independent preference gate, a rising SigLIP curve will not mean better images.
5. **Validate that the optimizer moves the policy** before long runs. Examples: a short overfit on a handful of prompts at the
   new sampler, with KL, clip fraction and reward rising on those prompts.

Lowering the learning rate (`window2_lr3e5_from15`) was not an informative intervention. The step size was not the
bottleneck, because the gradient carried almost no reward information.
